Operational objective. By the end of this section you can identify people and objects, ask what something is, follow basic instructions. The focus is useful performance, not isolated terminology.

Language system. Noun gender and number; definite and indefinite articles; hay; classroom commands.

Sound and delivery. Final consonants, especially s; contrast singular and plural without adding an English vowel.

Core principle. Notice meaning first, then form. Retrieve the new language aloud, compare it with the model, and repair one feature at a time.

Gender is part of the noun. Every Spanish noun has grammatical gender. Learn the article and noun as one unit: la palabra, not just palabra; el libro, not just libro. Grammatical gender is a word category and does not necessarily describe sex or identity.

The four definite articles. Use el with a masculine singular noun and la with a feminine singular noun. Their plurals are los and las: el libro → los libros; la palabra → las palabras.

Indefinite articles. Un and una mean a or an; unos and unas mean some. They must match the noun: un libro, una mesa, unos libros, unas mesas.

Patterns are clues, not guarantees. Nouns ending in -o are often masculine, while -a, -ción, -sión, and -dad are often feminine. These are useful clues, not rules without exceptions: el día, el problema, and la mano must be learned with their articles.

Agreement across the phrase. Articles, nouns, and descriptive adjectives agree in gender and number: un libro nuevo, una palabra nueva, los libros nuevos, las palabras nuevas. Some adjectives, such as importante, use the same singular form for both genders.
